GHOSTLINE

Hide and seek, played across a real square mile.

How to play

The idea

A few hunters chase everyone else. The ghosts just have to still be free when the clock runs out. Everything happens on foot, inside one square you choose on the map — a mile a side is about right for seven people and half an hour.

The pulse

Every couple of minutes the game gives every ghost away at once, as a circle on the hunters' map. How big that circle is depends on what you were doing at that instant:

  • Standing still — a tight, damning circle. A stationary signal is easy to pin down.
  • Walking — an ordinary blob.
  • Running — a wide smear, but it leaks which way you were heading.

So hiding in a bush for half an hour is the losing move. The pulse gets everyone moving, and it speeds up as the match goes on.

Charge and caches

Abilities cost charge, and charge is earned by walking — nothing else. Loot caches are scattered around the square; stand on one for a couple of seconds to open it. You can carry three items.

Getting caught

A hunter has to stay within the catch radius for three seconds. Caught ghosts join the hunt after a short freeze, so the net grows all match. In the last stretch the square shrinks and squeezes everyone together.

Going dark

Pocketing your phone to move untracked is the obvious cheat, so the game watches for it. A glance at a notification is free. Beyond that, your position is broadcast to the other side for as long as you are away, your charge drains, your cloak drops and you score nothing. Come back having covered more ground than a walk would explain and you are flagged: lit up for everyone, abilities locked.

The check does not trust your phone to own up. If a device stops reporting, it counts as dark whatever it claims afterwards.

Before you go out

  • Agree the boundary out loud as well as on the map — pavements, not fences.
  • Roads, private gardens and trespassing are not part of the game.
  • Keep the screen on. It is the whole bargain.
